# v5.1: review of `involved` organs (2026-10-22)

GUIDE_v5.md defines `involved` as an organ the disease itself directly damages: inflammation, infiltration, deposition that impairs
function, tumour, infarction, fibrosis, loss of function. It does not say what to do with congenital structural anomalies, and the
v5 labels often listed them as `involved`. The labels are meant to tell an oncologist which organs are already damaged before a
cancer drug is given. This review keeps `primary` as it is and checks every `involved` organ against two added rules.

## Drop an `involved` organ when its only support is one of these

1. **Congenital structural anomaly without lasting loss of function** of that organ: for example syndactyly, brachydactyly,
   clinodactyly, polydactyly, minor skeletal or facial dysmorphism; cleft lip or palate, dental anomalies, high palate; ear
   shape or position anomalies; strabismus, ptosis, hypertelorism and other eyelid or orbit anomalies; minor cardiac or renal
   malformations that do not impair function. These are form, not damage.
2. **Frequency from too few patients:** the organ is supported only by HPO counts from 1 or 2 patients (e.g. "1/1", "2/2"),
   with no established knowledge that the organ is damaged in most patients.

## Keep an `involved` organ when

- the anomaly itself causes lasting loss of function in a substantial share of patients: for example sensorineural hearing loss
  (otologic), visual impairment from optic atrophy, retinal dystrophy, cataract or coloboma involving the retina or optic nerve
  (ocular), structural heart disease needing treatment or causing heart failure (cardiovascular), renal hypoplasia or dysplasia
  with reduced kidney function (renal), progressive scoliosis, contractures, joint destruction or fractures (musculoskeletal),
  feeding failure needing a tube, intestinal malrotation or atresia (gastrointestinal);
- or the organ meets the GUIDE_v5.md `involved` conditions for another reason (inflammation, infiltration, fibrosis, tumour).

When in doubt, keep the organ.
